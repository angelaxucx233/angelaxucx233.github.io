Poetry

Soul Poems  ·  April 2026

Verse, Rage, Zither

Verse

Verse May sound terse… Wherein one might immerse And nurse A free mind, a free spirit Which, with a sigh and shudder lurches And serenely perches On a soul in crest– While form, eternally birthing hope; not yet besmirched by shadow, nor taste of the gloomy hearse.

Rage

They say Rage is a cage. I think not.
For a cage Does not blind the sight but only binds the night.
But Rage inhibits The sight, Makes wrong the right And with glee inhabits The body And drives away the light.

Zither

My dear, pick up your zither, Let it quiver Among the stars that shiver, with riffs, Yet unsung.
The young wanders whither Life is, as it were. Love beckons hither In scent and murmur
Malice which slithers And slurs. Old age that dithers A dirge.
And hope that withers Amidst war and winters And revives and arrives and thrives With spring.
And as your melodies blither Away into blurs Stars churn and yearn and burn And burst Into suns.

By Angela Xu. First published in 295 Magazine, Yale Asian American Cultural Center, 2026.
