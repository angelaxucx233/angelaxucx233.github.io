Gold

What is it about Gold I wonder That drives men mad with desire? Tinker, tailor, soldier, sailor All fall prey to her charm.
Is it her skin which glitters And casts her companion In halos? Or her form, molded Every which way As can be desired?
Is it the admiration she begets From princes and paupers, The awe From merchants and bankers, The camaraderie From socialites and sailors And envy From the worthless and worn?
Men sing, A rare jewel indeed is she! Let me hide her Under my cloak Settle her tranquilly In my castle tower. Other men may look, but They cannot possess.
Let my fingers trail Over her veins, And caress Gently her orbs. Let me grow old with her And my silvered hair Mingle with hers.
Oh, my beautiful one, She is even immortal! Then I must part from her, But never she from me.
I wonder how Gold feels Or how many lovers she has known. Was she cherished, Or ill-treated, Hidden at home, Or brought out abroad? Was she found, Bought, stolen, Or crafted from her lover’s bone Like our mother Eve?
Was she ever sad to lose her keepers, I wonder? Are their imprints also Forever inscribed In her molten heart?

By Angela Xu. First published in 295 Magazine, Yale Asian American Cultural Center, 2026.
