《诫子书》

A Letter of Counsel to My Son

Zhuge Liang  ·  227 AD

夫君子之行，静以修身，俭以养德。

As for the conduct of a cultivated person — quiet the mind to cultivate your person, practice simplicity to nurture your virtue.

非淡泊无以明志，非宁静无以致远。

For without being inwardly settled, one cannot have clarity of purpose, and without peace of mind one cannot journey far.

夫学须静也，才须学也，

非学无以广才，非志无以成学。

Indeed, for learning requires stillness, and talent requires learning.

For without learning, talent will remain undeveloped, and without purpose, learning will go unfulfilled.

淫慢则不能励精，险躁则不能治性。

For with excess and indolence, one cannot invigorate the inner faculties, and with impetuosity and agitation, one cannot govern one's nature.

年与时驰，意与日去，遂成枯落，

多不接世，悲守穷庐，将复何及！

Youth and years gallop away; aspirations recede with the days — and all eventually will wither and fail.

Most men do not meet and wrestle with the world in their time, sadly only to keep (rusting unburnished, in the words of Tennyson!) to their own meagre dwelling. By then, what can be reclaimed?!

Translation by Angela Xu. Original text from Zhuge Liang's Jie Zi Shu (诫子书), addressed to his son Zhuge Zhan.
