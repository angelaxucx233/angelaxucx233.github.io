《邹忌讽齐王纳谏》

Zou Ji Ridicules the King of Qi into Accepting Advice

Reign of King Wei of Qi  ·  356–320 BC

邹忌修八尺有余，而形貌昳丽。朝服衣冠，窥镜，谓其妻曰："我孰与城北徐公美？"

其妻曰："君美甚，徐公何能及君也？"

城北徐公，齐国之美丽者也。忌不自信，而复问其妾曰："吾孰与徐公美？"妾曰："徐公何能及君也？"

旦日，客从外来，与坐谈，问之客曰："吾与徐公孰美？"客曰："徐公不若君之美也。"

Zou Ji stood taller than 1.85 meters and was ruddy and handsome. One morning, as he donned his clothes and official's cap, he glanced in the mirror and asked his wife: "Who is more beautiful, Duke Xu in the City's north, or I?"

His wife replied: "My lord is the most beautiful! How can Duke Xu's beauty compare with thine?"

Now, Duke Xu of the City's north was known for his beauty in the State of Qi. Hence, Ji did not have confidence and again asked his concubine, "Who is more beautiful, Duke Xu or I?" His concubine replied: "How can Duke Xu's beauty exceed thine?"

The next day, a guest visited the house and sat talking with Ji. Ji asked his guest, "Who is more beautiful, Duke Xu or I?" His guest replied: "Duke Xu is not more beautiful than my lord."

明日徐公来，孰视之，自以为不如；窥镜而自视，又弗如远甚。

暮寝而思之，曰："吾妻之美我者，私我也；妾之美我者，畏我也；客之美我者，欲有求于我也。"

The next day, Duke Xu came to visit. He beheld the Duke and concluded that he himself was not as beautiful. He glanced in the mirror at himself and again knew that he was not as handsome as the Duke by a far stretch.

That evening in bed, he reflected thus: "That my wife declared me more beautiful, it is because she favors me; that my concubine declared me more beautiful, it is because she fears me; that my guest declared me more beautiful, it is because he desired to make request of me."

于是入朝见威王，曰："臣诚知不如徐公美。臣之妻私臣，臣之妾畏臣，臣之客欲有求于臣，皆以美于徐公。

今齐地方千里，百二十城，宫妇左右莫不私王，朝廷之臣莫不畏王，四境之内莫不有求于王。

由此观之，王之蔽甚矣。"

Then, he entered the court to see King Wei, saying: "Your servant knew that your servant is honestly not as beautiful as Duke Xu. Your servant's wife favors your servant; your servant's concubine fears your servant; your servant's guest desired to make request of your servant — hence they all declared that your servant was more beautiful than Duke Xu.

Today, within your realm of one thousand li and 120 cities — are there any among your wives and concubines and retainers who do not favor the King? Are there any among the ministers of the court who do not fear the King? Are there any within the four borders who do not wish to petition the King?

From this regard, the King is utterly blinded!"

王曰："善。"

乃下令："群臣吏民，能面刺寡人之过者，受上赏；上书谏寡人者，受中赏；能谤讥于市朝，闻寡人之耳者，受下赏。"

令初下，群臣进谏，门庭若市；数月之后，时时而间进；期年之后，虽欲言，无可进者。

The King replied, "Very well."

Hence, he issued a command: "Among our ministers and subjects, those who can criticize us outright in our presence will receive a high reward. Those who remonstrate us in writing will receive a moderate reward. Those who rebuke us in the public marketplace and word reaches our ears — they will receive a smaller reward."

When the command first went out, crowds of ministers came to remonstrate the King, and the gate was as the marketplace. A few months later, there were only a few claimants here and there. One year later, although the King desired remonstrators, there were none who came.

燕、赵、韩、魏闻之，皆朝于齐。

此所谓战胜于朝廷。

When the courts of Yan, Zhao, Han, and Wei heard of this, they all sent emissaries to the court of Qi.

This is called victory through court administration.

Translation by Angela Xu. Original text from the Zhanguo Ce (Stratagems of the Warring States), compiled c. 1st century BC.
