《出师表》

Memorial to the Emperor Upon Parting on an Expedition

Zhuge Liang  ·  227 AD

先帝创业未半而中道崩殂，今天下三分，益州疲弊，此诚危急存亡之秋也。

然侍卫之臣不懈于内，忠志之士忘身于外者，盖追先帝之殊遇，欲报之于陛下也。

The late Emperor's great task1 was not yet half complete when he died midway. Today, the realm stands divided into three and Yizhou2 is exhausted and worn; truly, this is a critical moment upon which our survival or extinction hangs.

And yet, (your) retainers and ministers are unflagging in their duties at court and (your) loyal officers of resolve forget their own lives on the battlefield — for recalling to mind the extraordinary favor shown to them by the late Emperor, they wish to repay to your Majesty (in loyal service).

诚宜开张圣听，以光先帝遗德，恢弘志士之气，

不宜妄自菲薄，引喻失义，以塞忠谏之路也。

Truly, (your Majesty) ought to open wide your imperial ears (to my counsel), and thus to honor and make known the inheritance of the late Majesty's virtue, thereby swelling and strengthening the hearts of your officers of resolve.

(Your Majesty) ought not to perversely diminish and view yourself lightly, and (thereby) let your words lose their soundness, and thus obstruct the intake of loyal counsel.

宫中府中，俱为一体；陟罚臧否，不宜异同。

若有作奸犯科及为忠善者，宜付有司论其刑赏，以昭陛下平明之理，不宜偏私，使内外异法也。

The Palace and the Government ought to be as one body; in promoting and sanctioning, in assigning merit or censure, there ought not to be differing standards.

As for those who commit treachery and transgress the law or those who act loyally and worthily — their cases ought to be committed to the appropriate officer to determine their punishment or reward according to your Majesty's just and enlightened principles; not swerving to the right nor to the left or showing partiality and thus causing the law of the inner (Palace) and outer courts (Government) to differ.

侍中、侍郎郭攸之、费祎、董允等，此皆良实，志虑忠纯，是以先帝简拔以遗陛下。

愚以为宫中之事，事无大小，悉以咨之，然后施行，必能裨补阙漏，有所广益。

Senior and Junior Palace Secretaries Guo Youzhi, Fei Yi, Dong Yun and the rest — all good and honest men whose hearts and minds are loyal and pure; they were handpicked by the late Emperor and bequeathed to your Majesty.

In my humble opinion — in the affairs of the Palace, whether big or small, consult these men on all matters, and execute (the decisions) afterwards. Surely, they can catch and remedy omissions and pitfalls and thus contribute much good.

将军向宠，性行淑均，晓畅军事，试用于昔日，先帝称之曰能，是以众议举宠为督。

愚以为营中之事，悉以咨之，必能使行阵和睦，优劣得所。

As to your general Xiang Chong, his nature and actions are just and steady; he is well-versed in military affairs. When he was formerly put to proof in command, the late Emperor had declared him capable, and he was thus promoted by common consent to become a Commander.

In my humble opinion, consult him on all military affairs. Surely, he can ensure harmony within the armies and assign the excellent and unworthy properly to their ranks.

亲贤臣，远小人，此先汉所以兴隆也；

亲小人，远贤臣，此后汉所以倾颓也。

先帝在时，每与臣论此事，未尝不叹息痛恨于桓、灵也。

侍中、尚书、长史、参军，此悉贞良死节之臣，愿陛下亲之信之，则汉室之隆，可计日而待也。

Draw close to virtuous ministers and keep far from petty men, for this is how the Former Han prospered;

Drawing close to petty men and keeping far from virtuous ministers — by this the Later Han had declined and fell.

When the late Emperor was alive, every time he spoke to your servant of these things, never once did he not sigh bitterly regarding the Emperors Heng and Ling.3

The Senior Palace Secretaries4, Senior Administrator5, Chief Aide6, Military Officer7 — all ministers who would guard their integrity to the death — wish your Majesty to draw near to and to put your trust in them, so that the number of days until restoration of the prosperity of the House of Han may be counted and confidently awaited.

臣本布衣，躬耕于南阳，苟全性命于乱世，不求闻达于诸侯。

先帝不以臣卑鄙，猥自枉屈，三顾臣于草庐之中，咨臣以当世之事，由是感激，遂许先帝以驱驰。

后值倾覆，受任于败军之际，奉命于危难之间，尔来二十有一年矣。

Your servant had been a man of plain cloth8 and was tilling the fields by my own hands in Nanyang, content only to preserve my own life during a time torn asunder in chaos rather than to seek fame and fortune among the feudal lords.

The late Emperor did not despise your servant as lowly and graciously condescended to seek out your servant three times in my thatched cottage, to consult your servant regarding the affairs of the time. Moved by this, I thereafter pledged myself to his late Majesty's whole-hearted service.

After this, as my time chanced the hour of topple and collapse9, I was assigned responsibilities at this juncture of military defeat and accepted the commission amidst danger and hardship — it has since been twenty-one years.

先帝知臣谨慎，故临崩寄臣以大事也。受命以来，夙夜忧叹，恐托付不效，以伤先帝之明；故五月渡泸，深入不毛。

今南方已定，兵甲已足，当奖率三军，北定中原，庶竭驽钝，攘除奸凶，兴复汉室，还于旧都。此臣所以报先帝而忠陛下之职分也。

至于斟酌损益，进尽忠言，则攸之、祎、允之任也。

His late Majesty knew your servant to be careful and discreet and for this reason entrusted your servant with the important affairs of state on his deathbed. Since I received the charge, I have anxiously sighed day and night for fear of not discharging my trust effectively and thus wounding the late Majesty's enlightened reputation; this is the reason why in May, your servant crossed the River Lu10, venturing deep into the barren wasteland.

Today, the southern region is stabilized and the soldiers and armory are well-supplied. (Now,) we ought to encourage and rally the three armies to stabilize the Central Plains to the north — that this slow horse and dull blade may be spent to the uttermost — uproot and rid the treacherous and wicked (rival power Cao-Wei), restore the House of Han, and return to the old capital. With this, your servant may repay the late Emperor and fulfill my loyalty to your Majesty's charge.

As to the deliberate weighing of positive and negative consequences, and offering full and frank advice — this is the duty of Youzhi, Yi, and Yun.

愿陛下托臣以讨贼兴复之效，不效，则治臣之罪，以告先帝之灵。

若无兴德之言，则责攸之、祎、允等之慢，以彰其咎；陛下亦宜自谋，以咨诹善道，察纳雅言，深追先帝遗诏。

臣不胜受恩感激。今当远离，临表涕零，不知所言。

May your Majesty entrust your servant to effect the chastisement of the usurpers. (Should I) not succeed, then punish your servant for his crime and account it thus to the spirit of the departed Emperor.

If there is no counsel which prospers virtue, then hold guilty the negligence of Youzhi, Yi, and Yun, and thus display their culpability. Your Majesty ought also to take counsel with yourself, to seek out the good path — examining and accepting worthy and proper counsel — to earnestly pursue the commission the late Emperor had bequeathed.

Your servant cannot overcome my heartfelt gratitude and deep feeling at receiving your Majesty's favor. Today, I must depart on a faraway journey. As I submit this memorial, I weep and know not what to say.

  1. ↑ The former Emperor Liu Bei wished to restore the weakened House of Han.
  2. ↑ Shu-Han's heartland, modern day Sichuan.
  3. ↑ The Emperors Heng and Ling favored eunuchs and gave them much influence and the court fell into corruption.
  4. ↑ Guo Youzhi and Fei Yi.
  5. ↑ Chen Zhen.
  6. ↑ Zhang Yi.
  7. ↑ Jiang Wan.
  8. ↑ Denotes commoner status.
  9. ↑ The former Emperor Liu Bei was disastrously defeated by Cao Cao at Dangyang Changbanpo in 208.
  10. ↑ In March 225, Zhuge Liang led troops across the River Lu (May 225) despite danger of illness to quell rebellions in the southern region of Shu-Han, which he succeeded in doing by the autumn of the same year.

Translation by Angela Xu. Original text from Zhuge Liang's Chu Shi Biao (出师表), 227 AD, addressed to Emperor Liu Shan of Shu Han.
